Are O4 and O7 independent lines of evidence?

An analysis of CancerHotspots and COSMIC against the SVIG-UK oncogenicity framework, and a proposal for capping the combined contribution of variant recurrence and hotspot evidence.

SummaryThe short answer

O4 and O7 are not fully independent, and the dependency is larger and more structural than the shared-cohort argument alone suggests. There are two distinct channels of double counting, and they call for different remedies.

  1. A shared-cohort channel. Just under half of the mutation observations that make a CancerHotspots residue statistically significant come from MSK‑IMPACT, which is also a GENIE contributing centre. For a typical hotspot, roughly half the observations behind O7 are the same patient observations an analyst then counts in GENIE for O4.
  2. A definitional channel. Every route to O7 through cancerhotspots.org in SVIG-UK v1.1 is gated on the count of the same amino-acid change — which is precisely what O4 measures. This holds irrespective of cohort overlap, and it is the finding we would put before the group first.
  3. The coupling is measured, not assumed. Using COSMIC as an independent stand-in for GENIE, 195 of the 198 amino-acid changes reaching O7_strong (98.5%) also reach O4_strong. At the top of the scale the two codes fire together almost deterministically.
  4. Capping Cancer Hotspots at supporting across the board is not the right fix. It would discard genuine positional evidence at the ~18% of hotspot residues that are mutated across many different substitutions — KRAS p.G12, NRAS p.Q61, TP53 p.R273.
  5. The conditional cap proposed by KRB is the right shape, it can be made operational, and its impact is small and targeted. It would leave the score of roughly 85% of hotspot changes untouched and drop only ten from +8 to +4, four of which are already covered by O1 and therefore unaffected in practice.

Section 1The question, and why it is not obvious

O4 and O7 are intended to capture different things. O4 measures recurrence and enrichment of a specific variant in a large somatic dataset. O7 asks whether the residue is a statistically significant mutational hotspot or lies in a critical functional region. On that reading they are separable lines of evidence, and Supplementary Figure 1 of SVIG-UK v1.1 permits them to be combined without restriction — up to +8 points.

The concern is that both are frequently satisfied by the same underlying patient observations. Cancer Hotspots is not a simple case-counting database; it applies a statistical model that accounts for mutability at a given amino acid and for gene- and position-specific mutation rates.1,2 But if the residue's significance is driven largely by the same cases that are counted in GENIE, then awarding +4 for O4 and +4 for O7 gives +8 points from what is, biologically, partly one signal of positive selection.

The guideline is alert to this. Its O7 section already ends with an instruction to exclude the overlapping cohorts:

“MSK and the TCGA should be excluded from this count to enable the use of Cancer Hotspots (O7) without double counting evidence.”

SVIG-UK v1.1, main text, O7

The intent is exactly the one under discussion. But the sentence sits at the end of a paragraph about in silico protein modelling and DECIPHER plots, so “this count” has no unambiguous antecedent; and read as an instruction to exclude MSK and TCGA cases from the O4 count it is not practicable, since TCGA is not in GENIE and the GENIE portal offers no per-centre view aligned to the CancerHotspots cohort. The mitigation exists on paper but cannot be executed at the bench.

This report sets out to establish, from the data, how large the overlap actually is, whether it matters, and what a workable remedy would look like.

Section 2What was analysed

Three resources were used, all at the level of the amino-acid change, which is the level at which SVIG-UK is written.

Cancer Hotspots

Both published releases were merged: Chang et al. 20161 (459 residues) and Chang et al. 20182 (1,110 single-residue positions across 24,592 tumours, plus 55 in-frame indel regions). For each hotspot residue the resource publishes the total mutation count and the count for each individual amino-acid change — for example NRAS p.Q61 carries 422 mutations distributed as Q61R:204 | Q61K:142 | Q61L:46 | Q61H:27 | Q61P:2 | Q61*:1.

Critically, the 2018 release also reports, for every residue, how that total splits between MSK‑IMPACT and retrospective public cohorts (largely TCGA). This split is what makes the O4/O7 overlap directly measurable rather than a matter of argument. It was verified that the two components reconstitute the published total for all 3,004 rows.

The primary analysis set is the 2018 single-residue table restricted to protein-coding positions: 1,024 residues and 2,918 distinct amino-acid changes. Splice hotspots (86 positions) are excluded because O7 does not apply to them, and within the analysis set a further 247 nonsense or stop-loss changes are marked ineligible for O7, which is reserved for missense and small in-frame variants. That leaves 1,736 changes that score O7 at some strength from cancerhotspots.org.

COSMIC Cancer Mutation Census

GENIE was not available for this analysis. SVIG-UK explicitly permits COSMIC as an alternative resource for O4, and the Cancer Mutation Census (v104) publishes a per-substitution sample count drawn from a cohort that is largely not the CancerHotspots cohort. It is used here to measure how often O4 and O7 fire on the same variant — not to make classification calls on individual variants.

SVIG-UK Canonical Variants List

The O1 list (Supplementary Table 3) was used to establish which of the affected variants are already covered by standalone evidence, and therefore unaffected by any change to O4/O7 scoring.

Data integrity

The analysis is built on the supplementary workbooks published with the papers, but an analyst applying O7 reads the website. The parsed workbook was therefore cross-checked against a cancerhotspots.org API export: all 3,004 amino-acid changes match, with 100% agreement on both the per-change counts and the residue totals. The figures modelled here are the figures seen at the bench.

Section 3How much of Cancer Hotspots is MSK?

Across the 1,024 protein-coding hotspot residues, 25,814 mutation observations underpin the hotspot calls. Of these, 12,087 (46.8%) come from MSK‑IMPACT and 13,727 (53.2%) from retrospective public cohorts.

The degree of overlap is measurable per residue, not constant.

Section 4O7’s thresholds are variant-level, not positional

This is the finding that does not depend on any cohort-overlap argument, and it is visible on the face of Supplementary Table 1. Read the cancerhotspots.org tiers as an analyst applies them:

Every tier is gated on the recurrence count of the exact same amino-acid change. The supporting tier is defined on nothing else. The positional count only ever arbitrates between strong and moderate — it never, on its own, admits O7.

O4’s missense thresholds are > 10 entries for strong and 5–10 for moderate. So the ≥ 10 same-change requirement that lifts O7 above supporting sits almost exactly on O4’s strong threshold. A variant scoring O7_strong from cancerhotspots.org has, by construction, satisfied a recurrence test nearly identical to the one that gave it O4_strong — on a patient set that is around 47% shared.

Conceptually, O4 and O7 can be independent. As currently specified, the cancerhotspots.org route to O7 does not measure the positional signal in isolation.

Section 5How positional are hotspots in practice?

For O7 to carry genuinely positional information, a residue needs to be mutated to several different amino acids. It frequently is not.

The more informative measure is the fraction of a residue’s mutations accounted for by its commonest substitution. The median is 0.69 and the mean 0.70. Classifying residues on that fraction:

Two residues in five are ≥ 80% dominated by a single change; for those, O7 adds little to O4. But the remaining 18.5% are the opposite case, and they are the reason not to cap Cancer Hotspots across the board. KRAS p.G12 carries ten distinct substitutions over 2,175 mutations, the commonest (G12D) accounting for only 34.8%. NRAS p.Q61 carries six over 422 (top 48.3%); TP53 p.R273 six over 609 (top 41.2%). At these residues the hotspot designation survives comfortably without the variant under assessment, and O7 is telling the analyst something O4 is not.

Section 6Testing the coupling against a second database

The argument so far is structural. It can be tested. COSMIC CMC provides an O4-style recurrence count drawn from a largely different cohort; 2,570 of the 2,918 hotspot changes (88.1%) match a COSMIC substitution.

The recurrence signal that drives O7 and the recurrence signal that would drive O4 are strongly correlated even across two largely different cohorts. This is the point worth putting to the group: removing the MSK overlap entirely would not make these two codes independent, because they are measuring the same underlying phenomenon — positive selection on a specific residue change.

The trade-off to weigh

A four-point reduction is not cosmetic. Against Figure 1C of the guideline it can move a variant from Likely oncogenic (6–9) to VUS (0–5) where the rest of the workup is thin. That is the judgement the group needs to make, and it is the reason for recommending the permissive calibration of the independence test below rather than the strict one.

Section 8Proposed amendment

This is the amendment suggested by KRB, with the “demonstrably supported by multiple independent amino acid changes” clause turned into a test that can be applied at the bench from the same web page the analyst is already reading.

Proposed footnote to the O4 × O7 cell, Supplementary Figure 1

Restriction: where O7 is supported by cancerhotspots.org recurrence of the same amino-acid change used to support O4, the sum of points from O4 and O7 is limited to 4 pts (strong). O4 and O7 may be combined in full where the hotspot residue is supported by ≥ 10 further mutations arising from other amino-acid changes.

The leave-one-variant-out test

Subtract the count for the variant under assessment’s own amino-acid change from the residue total. The residue provides independent evidence if the residual is ≥ 10 mutations and at least one other amino-acid change at that residue is itself recurrent. The ≥ 10 threshold deliberately mirrors the count SVIG-UK already uses to lift O7 above supporting.

Two calibrations were modelled. The permissive test (another change with ≥ 2 mutations) leaves 773 of 1,736 O7-scoring changes uncapped (44.5%); the strict test (another change with ≥ 10 mutations, independently meeting the guideline’s own bar) leaves 599 (34.5%). The permissive test is recommended, because the strict test additionally penalises residues such as KIT p.D816 where the positional signal is real but the absolute counts are small.

The rule correctly lets it combine. EGFR p.L858R is the clean opposite: one change, 144 of 144, four-fifths of it MSK. There is no positional evidence there that is not L858R’s own recurrence.

Two further points for the group

The precedent for a cap already exists. Supplementary Figure 1, note 6 reads:

“Restriction: Sum of points awarding from combining O6 and O7 is limited to 4 pts (strong) to avoid double counting key attributes of critical domains / residues which are also often measured by in silico tools.”

SVIG-UK v1.1, Supplementary Figure 1, note 6

The proposed O4 + O7 cap is the same construction applied to a more strongly correlated pair. The O4 × O7 cell of that matrix carries no footnote at all.

The existing mitigation should be revised, not left standing alongside a cap. The “MSK and the TCGA should be excluded from this count” sentence quoted in Section 1 is ambiguous in its placement and impracticable as written. If a cap is adopted, that sentence should be replaced rather than duplicated.

Section 9Recommendation

  1. Adopt the conditional cap, with the leave-one-variant-out test as the operational definition of independence, added as a footnote to the O4 × O7 cell of Supplementary Figure 1 in the form given above.
  2. Do not cap Cancer Hotspots at supporting across the board. It would under-credit the ~18% of residues carrying real positional evidence and weaken classification of genuinely position-driven drivers such as KRAS p.G12. The conditional cap achieves the same protection while changing the score of only 15% of hotspot changes.
  3. Revise the “MSK and the TCGA should be excluded from this count” sentence, which is ambiguous and not practicable as written.
  4. State explicitly that O7 via cancerhotspots.org is, as written, a variant-level test, and that the positional argument requires evidence of multiple distinct changes at the residue.

Section 10Limitations and open points

COSMIC is a stand-in for GENIE, not a substitute

GENIE was not available, so the O4 side of Sections 6 and 7 is modelled on COSMIC CMC v104. COSMIC and GENIE differ in composition, ascertainment and duplicate handling, and the guideline warns that COSMIC thresholds should be higher. The COSMIC analysis is evidence about how tightly O4-style and O7-style recurrence track each other, not a prediction of the exact GENIE count any individual variant would return.

Repeating Section 6 against GENIE is the obvious next step, and it would answer a question nothing else can. GENIE reports the contributing centre for every sample, which would allow the O4 count to be computed with and without MSK for each variant — that is, it would show directly how many variants change O4 tier when the guideline’s own exclusion is applied. That analysis decides between three options: if excluding MSK changes little, the existing mitigation suffices and needs only clearer wording; if it changes a great deal, the cap is the necessary remedy. An access request is in progress.

Version currency and choice

CancerHotspots v2 (2018, 24,592 tumours) predates current GENIE releases. The MSK fraction measured here is the MSK share within the hotspot resource, which is the quantity that determines whether the hotspot call and the GENIE count rest on the same patients — but the absolute overlap with a present-day GENIE query will differ. Separately, the two CancerHotspots releases differ substantially: 459 residues in v1 against 1,024 in v2, with only 252 shared. The website serves v2. Whichever is used should be version-stamped in the report, as the guideline already requires for GENIE.

Haematological coverage is a real gap

The guideline notes in general terms that cancerhotspots.org coverage is more limited for haematological malignancies. Quantified against named targets, JAK2 p.V617F, MPL p.W515, CALR exon 9, NPM1 exon 12, ASXL1 and SETBP1 are absent from both the SNV and indel tables of CancerHotspots v2. O7 is simply unavailable through this resource for several of the most important haematological drivers, which may be worth stating explicitly in the guidance rather than leaving to the analyst to discover.

Scope of the analysis set

Splice hotspots (86 residues) are excluded, as O7 does not apply to them. Nonsense and stop-loss changes (247) are marked ineligible for O7, which is reserved for missense and small in-frame variants and cannot be combined with O2; their counts still contribute to the published residue totals, as they do on the website. The 55 in-frame indel regions are ranges rather than single residues, so the “distinct changes at a position” construction does not transfer, and they are excluded from the tier analysis. Protein-level matching to COSMIC failed for 11.9% of changes, largely because the two resources use different reference transcripts — MYD88 p.L265P appears as p.L273P in COSMIC, and GNAS p.R201 does not join at all.

A textual discrepancy worth correcting

The main text permits O7 at strong for “> 50 occurrences at the same position”; Supplementary Table 1 says “≥ 50 entries”. These differ at exactly 50. This analysis uses the Supplementary Table wording, which is the operational table.

Section 11What could be done next, and what it would need

None of what follows is required to act on the recommendation in Section 9 — the case for the conditional cap rests on the evidence already presented. These are the analyses that would strengthen it, close a gap it leaves open, or extend the same reasoning to parts of the guideline this work did not reach.

The one that would settle the question outright

Everything in Sections 6 and 7 models the O4 side on COSMIC, because GENIE was not available. GENIE carries a contributing-centre identifier on every sample, so with the release itself the decisive quantity stops being inferred and becomes directly measurable: for each hotspot amino-acid change, does it still meet the O4 count threshold once MSK‑IMPACT samples are removed?

That converts the argument from “the two codes correlate at ρ = 0.83 across cohorts” into “n of the 198 changes reaching O7_strong lose O4_strong when the shared patients are taken out” — which is the number a committee will actually want in front of it. It also replaces the MSK fraction measured inside CancerHotspots (46.8%) with the true overlap between a present-day GENIE query and the hotspot call, and it decides between three outcomes: if excluding MSK changes little, the guideline’s existing mitigation suffices and needs only clearer wording; if it changes a great deal, the cap is the necessary remedy; if it changes a moderate amount, the conditional cap proposed here is the proportionate one.

Data required

An AACR Project GENIE release (v17.x or current): data_mutations_extended.txt and data_clinical_sample.txt, which carry SEQ_ASSAY_ID and CENTER per sample. Free to registered users through Synapse (syn7222066) on acceptance of the terms of use — no cost, but registration and a data-use agreement are required. Roughly a day of work once the file is in hand: the existing pipeline takes it with one new script in place of the COSMIC extraction step.

Reproducibility

The full analysis is held as a version-controlled repository: source workbooks unmodified, a numbered analysis pipeline, and every table in this report reproducible from a single run. Deliverable tables are provided as TSV and as a combined Excel workbook. The COSMIC archive, the counts derived from it and any GENIE data are held outside the repository under their respective licences and are not redistributed; the extraction script regenerates them from a licensed download.

Integrity checks are asserted rather than assumed: the per-change counts must sum to the published residue total, the MSK and retrospective components must reconstitute that total, and the parsed workbook is cross-checked against the live cancerhotspots.org export.
